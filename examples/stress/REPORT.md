# diagram v0.1: stress-test report

I built 13 example diagrams, rendered them to PNG/HTML, and had a graphic-designer agent review them in two rounds. Sources are in this folder and renders are in `out/`.

## Verdict per use case

The designer scored each render 1–5 for readability, hierarchy, aesthetics and fit for its audience.

| # | Use case | How it was built | Designer (R/H/A/F) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Multi-system flow (tech) | native | 2/3/3/3 | ✅ The model fits. The output is a 9:1 ribbon and edges cut through nodes. |
| 02 | Project status | native (groups as columns) | 4/3/3/3 | ✅ Usable. Parallel edges run through nodes, and the RAG headline has no weight. |
| 03 | Gantt (PM) | **faked** (pinned x/y/w/h, generated by `gen.py`) | 2/2/2/1 | ❌ No time axis, gridlines, progress fill or today line. Labels split mid-word ("Toda/y"). Not interactive. |
| 04 | QA test cases | native | 2/2/2/2 | ⚠️ The flow is fine, but test-case links become spaghetti. Needs a "test case → path" primitive. |
| 05 | Risk heat-map | **faked** (25 pinned boxes) | 3/3/3/3 | ⚠️ It works but takes 48 generated lines. Dots overlap and fills are too pale. |
| 06 | Decisions board | stickies in groups | 3/2/3/2 | ⚠️ Everything is one text block: no title, body or footer, no status chip, no avatar. |
| 07 | RACI | **faked** (pinned pills) | 4/3/3/4 | ⚠️ Best of the fakes, but it reads as floating buttons, not a table. |
| 08 | Teams & people | `person` nodes in groups | 2/3/3/2 | ⚠️ No avatars or person cards, and edge labels collide. |
| 09 | Big system (38 nodes) | native | 1/2/2/1 | ❌ 8422×1144 px. Unreadable, with no entry point. |
| 10 | Onboarding tour via `--base` diffs | native (4 files) | 4→3/3/3/4 | ⚠️ Each step reads well, but every step re-lays out, so the mental map breaks. |
| 11 | Swimlane handoff | groups | 4/3/3/3 | ⚠️ The groups aren't real lanes. |
| 12 | Workflow pipeline | native | 3/2/3/3 | ⚠️ No status strip, slots or avatar badge, and the blocker edge runs through cards. |
| 13 | Project overview card | grid + text | 2/1/2/1 | ❌ No stat tiles, progress bar or chips, and the hierarchy is upside down. |

**Strengths:** the text format, LLM ops, the comment → prompt → apply loop (tested: chatty replies parse, batches are atomic, a bad batch is rejected with exit 1), git diffs, and the HTML export.
**Weaknesses:** anything that is a *card*, *table* or *timeline* rather than a box-and-arrow diagram.

## Bug found and fixed

`diagram png/svg --base` emitted a duplicate `stroke-width` attribute on added or changed **groups**. The SVG was invalid XML, so `png` failed with *"attribute 'stroke-width' is already defined"*. The fix is in `crates/core/src/render.rs`, with a regression test `diffed_groups_are_valid_svg` in `lib.rs`. The test fails without the fix and passes with it.

## Other renderer defects observed

1. **Edges ignore obstacles.** They pass through nodes, groups and labels, and pinned nodes aren't treated as obstacles at all.
2. **Parallel and duplicate edges overlap.** There's no bundling and no port assignment.
3. **Aspect ratio is unbounded.** LR chains grow to 7–9:1, and nothing switches direction or wraps rows.
4. **Word wrapping splits words** in narrow pinned nodes ("Launc/h").
5. **Labels collide** with other labels and edges.
6. **Custom hex colours are always tinted.** There's no solid fill, so a solid dark dot is impossible.
7. **Block glyphs render as hatching** in PNG (resvg font fallback), so ▓░ can't stand in for a progress bar.
8. **Content inside a node has no structure** and is always centred.

## The big-diagram / newcomer problem

I prototyped two things on top of the existing engine, without changing it:

- **An auto-collapsed overview** (`09-big-platform.overview.diagram` → `out/09-overview.png`): each group becomes one node, and edges between groups are aggregated with a ×N count. The 38 nodes and 44 edges become 8 readable boxes. It was derived from the JSON model in about 15 lines, so it can become a native feature.
- **A tour viewer** (`make_tour.py` + `tour_template.html` → `out/09-big-platform-tour.html`):
  - narrated steps driven by a small `tour.json`;
  - dim and zoom to each step's focus on a **stable** layout;
  - click a node to see what it receives from and sends to;
  - `/` to search, ← → to step, Esc to explore freely.

  It works in Chrome on desktop and phone with no JS errors.

The designer's verdict was *"partly solved"*. Steps that zoom into a group are good. What's still wrong:

- **Step 0 shows the full map**, which is unreadable. It should show the collapsed overview.
- **The viewer can't stay readable on a wide layout.** When a node's neighbours span the whole width, the zoom stays small.
- **Group fills don't dim** along with their children.
- **Focus and search leave the step context**, and there's no breadcrumb back.
- **On a phone, a left-to-right layout becomes a thumbnail.**

### What the product should be (recommended)

1. **Semantic zoom as the default.** Big diagrams open on the collapsed overview. Clicking a group expands it in place, and other groups stay collapsed with aggregated edges. There's a minimum readable text size (~11 px): if fitting the screen would go below it, show the collapsed level instead.
2. **Tours as a first-class DSL statement**, stored and reviewed with the diagram:
   ```diagram
   step "Getting documents in" on ingest, email_in: Uploads and parsed e-mails are stored…
   ```
   Each step means "expand these groups + focus + caption". Comments can be attached to steps, so a newcomer's question becomes a review thread.
3. **Focus+context** (dim the rest, zoom to fit) for click, search and steps, with a breadcrumb back to the tour.

## Recommended engine work, ranked

1. **A structured card content model.** Slots (eyebrow/number, title, body, footer-left/right), a status strip, chips, a badge or avatar, a stat number, a progress bar, and left alignment. This closes most of the gap on 05–08, 12 and 13.
2. **Obstacle-aware orthogonal routing** with ports, bundling, stroke weight for ×N, and label collision avoidance.
3. **Semantic zoom, plus `step` tours** (above).
4. **Native `table` and `timeline` blocks.** `table` covers RACI and the heat-map. `timeline` covers the Gantt: time axis, lanes, progress fill, milestones, dependencies, a today line and weekend shading. Interactivity (drag to reschedule) comes from the editor operating on the timeline model.
5. **Responsive direction**: lay out top-to-bottom on narrow viewports, and wrap long chains into rows.
6. **A richer comment lifecycle** (open → discussing → agreed/rejected → queued → sent), to match SpecDeck.
